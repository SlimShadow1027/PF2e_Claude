# Pantheon and cosmology

Gods and cosmology **as this world understands them**. What is true across every world in the
universe goes in `worlds/UNIVERSE.md` instead — a power named here appears in this world
only, and the distinction is the most commonly broken rule in the shared layer.

## What the Verge thinks it is

There is no sun and the sky is not dark. Light here comes from the sky itself, evenly,
without a source anyone can point at, and it dims and brightens with the drift cycle rather
than with any day. The Verge has a word for night and uses it for the span of Stillness.

**Down** is real and is not a direction you can walk; it is what is below the shards, and
the Shoreward Watch will tell you there is no bottom. **Up** is where the arrivals came from
and is equally unwalkable. Both of these are agreed on by everyone and explained by nobody.

The dead go down. Not to the underside — further. Nothing here claims to know where, and the
confident answers are all recent and all sold.

## The powers worshipped here

### The Keeping

Not a god, originally: the practice of keeping the count. It has been worshipped for long
enough that the distinction has stopped mattering, and the Office of the Count is careful not
to encourage it. Its adherents hold that the world holds together because it is being
counted, and that an unbroken record is a load-bearing thing.

A heresy within it holds that the count is what broke the world, and that each year recorded
is another year it cannot heal.

### The Shore

Worshipped by the Watch, and by anyone who works an edge. Not personified and not asked for
anything; acknowledged, in the way weather is acknowledged. The Shore is credited with
deciding what falls and what holds, and the one rite it has is performed before going over,
by a person alone, and is not described to anyone who has not.

### The Three Names

The woman who began the Reckoning, whose name is given three different ways in three
different places (`HISTORY.md`, 1–4 VR). All three names are venerated, by three traditions
that each hold the other two to be errors. Each of the three is a complete religion with its
own calendar of observances, all of them keyed to the same drift cycle, which means they
celebrate on the same days and will not do it together.

### The direction

Not a religion and increasingly behaving like one. See `FACTIONS.md`.

## What this world does not claim

- **It does not name whatever took the Strand apart.** The deep past says something did; this
  world has no name for it that another world would recognise, and `UNIVERSE.md` leaves it
  unnamed for that reason.
- **It does not know what the Verge was before it broke.** The commonest answer — that it was
  a world like the one the arrivals described — is a guess built entirely out of the
  arrivals' accounts, and the Verge's own records do not reach back that far.
