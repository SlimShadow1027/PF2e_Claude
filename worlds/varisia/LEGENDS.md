# Legends

How the events in `CHRONICLE.md` are *told* in this world: exaggerated, misattributed,
politically edited, or plain wrong. When an NPC refers to a past event, they speak from
here, not from the chronicle.

Keep the two separate on purpose. A player hearing their old character's story told wrong
— and knowing it is wrong — is most of what a shared world is for.

Entries may carry a `- **Date:**` line, in which case `as-of` gates them like chronicle
entries. A legend with no date is treated as always current.

## The Roderic legends

> **Read this first.** Every entry below is a **legend**, not an event. None of it is in
> `CHRONICLE.md` and none of it is true until the campaign that owns Roderic says so.
>
> Roderic is a **living player character in another of this player's campaigns**, run in a
> separate session on their own machine. That campaign is *the real story* and it is being
> uncovered over time. **This session cannot read it.** The player asked for the myth to be
> invented here, with the explicit licence that it *"can be rectified or ignored as the other
> campaign continues."*
>
> So these are written to be **wrong in specific, correctable ways**. Where the real story
> contradicts one, the contradiction is the point: strike the legend, do not strike the
> history. A legend that turns out to be false stays in this file with a note, because in
> Varisia people will go on repeating it.

### What the other campaign has established

Carried across by hand from the player, 2026-09-30. The only facts here that are real.

- Roderic is **alive now**, not a historical figure.
- **Commander / Fighter**, dual-class — the same variant Karsa uses.
- **Second-in-command of the 5th squad** of a mercenary company.
- He lives in a city of **dark-grey legality**, where much of what would be crime elsewhere
  is simply how business is done.
- He was the **last of his first company to survive**. He was left holding its debts, and his
  contract was **sold** to the mercenary group that now owns it.

That last point is the engine of every legend below. **A man whose contract is an asset has
a reputation that is also an asset**, and reputations that are assets get embellished by
whoever owns them.

### Legend 1 — "Roderic bought the Cove"

**As told in Roderic's Cove, by the salvage syndicate.** That a mercenary officer named
Roderic settled the syndicate's debts a generation ago and took the naming of the place as
payment, and that the Cove has been solvent ever since.

**Why it is told:** it makes the syndicate's authority inherited rather than seized, and it
explains the name without anyone having to know anything about the man.

**Where it fails, and Karsa can find this:** the syndicate's own ledgers do not go back a
generation. They go back **eleven years**. Nobody in the Cove over forty remembers the place
being called anything else, which means either the name is much older than the story, or the
story is much newer than the name.

### Legend 2 — "Roderic was the only one who came back"

**As told in the Cove's two taverns, and believed.** That Roderic went down into the sea
caves with a company at his back and came out alone, and that this is why the caves are
dredged from a boat and never entered on foot.

**Why it is told:** because it is the shape of a true thing. Something did happen down there,
and somebody did come back alone.

**Where it fails:** the surviving detail attaches to the **wrong event**. Being the last of
his company to survive is a real fact about Roderic — the other campaign establishes it — but
it happened to a *mercenary company*, in a *city*, and it had nothing to do with a cave. The
legend has taken the true shape of the man and welded it onto the local landmark, which is
what legends do.

**This is the useful one.** If the two campaigns connect, this is the seam: the Cove's story
is a **distorted echo of an event Roderic actually lived through somewhere else entirely.**

### Legend 3 — "Roderic shut the door"

**As told by nobody in public, and by two people in private.** That the door under the cliff
was open once, that Roderic closed it, and that the town grew up around the job of keeping it
closed — the dredging being a cover for a watch.

**Why it is told:** the two people who tell it are the Cove's harbourmaster and an
eighty-year-old net-mender, and they do not know each other well enough to have agreed on it.
Two independent sources is the most interesting thing in the Cove.

**Where it fails:** the door is **Thassilonian**, which puts it ten thousand years before any
living mercenary. Roderic cannot have built it and is very unlikely to have shut it.

**Where it might not fail:** he could have *found* it. He could have told someone. The
harbourmaster's version says Roderic "closed it", but the net-mender's says he "**paid to have
it forgotten**" — and those are different crimes.

### Legend 4 — "There is no Roderic"

**As told by the Cove's own clerk, who is the closest thing the place has to an archivist,
and who is tired.** That "Roderic" is a corruption of a Varisian word, that the name has been
back-formed into a founder who never existed, and that the whole question is a waste of
everyone's time.

**Why it is told:** because the clerk has looked, and found exactly what Karsa is going to
find — two accounts of the founding, three of the man, and no primary source at all.

**Where it fails:** it is the one legend that would be disproved by simply meeting him.

### The pattern, for whoever runs this

The four legends are mutually exclusive on purpose, and they distort in four different ways:

| Legend | The distortion |
|---|---|
| 1 — bought the Cove | **Invented wholesale**, for a living, by people who benefit |
| 2 — only one who came back | **True detail, wrong event** — the most likely to survive contact with the real story |
| 3 — shut the door | **Chronologically impossible, but pointing at something real** |
| 4 — no such man | **Correct method, wrong conclusion** — good research, insufficient sources |

Karsa's research should be able to demolish 1 and 4 within the campaign. **2 and 3 should
survive to the end**, because they are the ones the other campaign gets to resolve.

**Nothing above asserts who Roderic is.** That is deliberate, and it should stay that way.
