# Stranger in a Strange Land

- **Slug:** stranger-in-a-strange-land
- **Created:** 2026-11-25
- **World:** none
- **Era:** not yet decided (original world; its own calendar to be named at world-build)
- **Start date:** 1 Abadius 4725 AR
- **Shape:** sandbox, long campaign — no overarching plot at the start; one is allowed to grow out of the enemies the protagonist makes and the dominoes they knock over. Local hero's journey: start small and local, widen as the protagonist's reputation does.
- **Expected level range:** 2 to 20 — the protagonist **starts at level 2**
- **Advancement:** milestone, fast at first — levels 2–5 every one or two sessions; levels 6–10 every two to four sessions; 11–20 slower again, pace to be set when we get there
- **Genre:** high fantasy × war, with monster hunting at the centre
- **Tone:** heroic, leaning pulpy — a local hero's journey; consequences are real but not punishingly realistic
- **Lethality:** between 1 and 2, rising with level — at low levels defeat is capture, injury or a setback rather than death; as levels climb, death becomes possible but is always telegraphed hard and avoidable. Revisit at level 6 and level 11.
- **Setting:** fully original world, in the spirit of *Ranger's Apprentice* — campy, unmistakable stand-ins for real-world and RPG-familiar cultures (a Norse raider nation; kingdoms that are obviously England, Scotland, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Japan and so on). Original names throughout: nothing taken from the books. Standalone (`World: none`) for now; world-hopping may come up later.

## Protagonist framing

Why is this character the one who acts?

**The stranger.** A competent adventurer from another world — a world much like
Golarion — pulled into this one. Ancestry is one this world also has (probably human, maybe
dwarf); character details are decided later (`system/02-character-creation.md`).

The core fantasy is **disparity**. In their own world the protagonist was one capable
adventurer among many. Here they are close to a demigod: this world's ordinary soldiers,
champions and beasts are measured on a much lower scale, and what the protagonist does with
a sword (or whatever they wield) looks impossible to the locals. The player's touchstone: "a
Pathfinder martial character dropped in a 5e world setting". **This world's magic, by
contrast, is potent** — its wizards, priests and great monsters are where real danger lives.

How the disparity shows up in play (to confirm in RULES_DELTAS.md at the end of intake):
ordinary natives are built low (level −1 to 2 published stat blocks, reskinned); crowds of them
fall fast; the encounter budget is spent on native casters, monsters and the occasional
champion, so fights stay real while the protagonist still feels enormous.

## Party structure

- **Characters the player controls:** 1 to start
- **Characters the GM runs:** 0 to start
- **Ally status:** none yet — the arc is planned, not given:
  1. **Solo** at the start, with the safety nets in RULES_DELTAS.md.
  2. **Quickly earns a simple companion NPC** (a sidekick: one job, few decisions).
  3. That companion **grows into a full GM-run NPC**, built as a full PC.
  4. Eventually **a small squad the player leads**, making the strategic decisions and the
     tactical calls; the GM runs the individuals' personalities, the player commands.
     Mechanics for the squad (troupe play vs. commanded allies) to be decided when it arrives.
