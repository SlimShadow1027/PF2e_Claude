# Stranger in a Strange Land

- **Slug:** stranger-in-a-strange-land
- **Created:** 2026-11-25
- **World:** none
- **Era:** not yet decided (original world; its own calendar to be named at world-build)
- **Start date:** 1 Abadius 4725 AR
- **Shape:** sandbox, long campaign — no overarching plot at the start; one is allowed to grow out of the enemies the protagonist makes and the dominoes they knock over. Local hero's journey: start small and local, widen as the protagonist's reputation does.
- **Expected level range:** 2 to 20 — the protagonist **starts at level 2**
- **Advancement:** milestone, fast at first — levels 2–5 every one or two sessions; levels 6–10 every two to four sessions; 11–20 slower again, pace to be set when we get there
- **Genre:** high fantasy × war, with monster hunting at the centre
- **Tone:** heroic, leaning pulpy — a local hero's journey; consequences are real but not punishingly realistic
- **Lethality:** between 1 and 2, rising with level — at low levels defeat is capture, injury or a setback rather than death; as levels climb, death becomes possible but is always telegraphed hard and avoidable. Revisit at level 6 and level 11.
- **Setting:** fully original world, in the spirit of *Ranger's Apprentice* — campy, unmistakable stand-ins for real-world and RPG-familiar cultures (a Norse raider nation; kingdoms that are obviously England, Scotland, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Japan and so on). Original names throughout: nothing taken from the books. Standalone (`World: none`) for now; world-hopping may come up later.

## The pitch

Chosen by the player on 2026-11-25 from three offered: **B, then C**, with **A kept for later.**

### Opening — Monster Country (pitch B)

A frontier march in **Hohenwald**, a Germanic-Scottish highland stand-in nation that distrusts
magic. Behind it, the hills were blasted by an old magic war and keep spilling monsters. A
monster-hunters' lodge drags the stranger off the battlefield, mistaking them for one of
their own gone missing. Bounties and frequent hunts; lodge ranks to climb. A **rival guild**
has been luring monsters down from the hills to keep bounties high, and the valley-killing
beast that the lodge will not touch is **the monster the stranger is not ready for yet**.

### The turn — into the arena (pitch C), as the player has asked

The player has set this transition themselves: the stranger **defeats the too-strong
monster**, which the rival guild lured into the valley, then **collapses after the fight and
is captured**, ending up sold into slavery in the arena of the **Vessarine Dominion**, an evil
stand-in empire built on Rome and Italy. From there: the arena master who wants a champion,
fame as currency, and escape or revolt.

Because the player wrote this arc, it is a **destination, not a script**. When and how the
capture happens is built in play, and the player keeps every rewind and veto.

### Saved for later — The Burning Field (pitch A)

Not canon yet. It is kept as a future story beat or encounter. **Albrecht**, a lawful kingdom
with church-licensed war-mages (an England/France stand-in), at war with the **Hrimmark**
raiders (Norse). A battlefield where both sides want to claim the stranger, a deserter
crossbowman, a chained fire-drake, and a jarl's plan to break the truce at the river fords.

## Premise and stakes

Sandbox: there is deliberately no villain plot at the start. The backdrop is this:

- **What is wrong with the world:** the gods keep a **very unstable, fragile peace**, and the
  nations that worship them sit on the brink of war. Every border incident is a spark.
- **Who is causing it:** nobody yet, as far as the protagonist knows. The pressure comes from
  the gods' rivalries working through their nations. Someone, or something, **dragged the
  protagonist here for a purpose**; who, and why, is left to emerge in play.
- **What happens if nobody stops it:** open war between the nations, and perhaps between the
  gods themselves. Left deliberately vague until play makes it specific.

The eventual story grows from the enemies the protagonist makes and the dominoes they knock
over. The pitches below propose a starting region and a first local problem, not a plot.

## Protagonist framing

Why is this character the one who acts?

**The stranger.** A competent adventurer from another world — a world much like
Golarion — pulled into this one. Ancestry is one this world also has (probably human, maybe
dwarf); character details are decided later (`system/02-character-creation.md`).

The core fantasy is **disparity**. In their own world the protagonist was one capable
adventurer among many. Here they are close to a demigod: this world's ordinary soldiers,
champions and beasts are measured on a much lower scale, and what the protagonist does with
a sword (or whatever they wield) looks impossible to the locals. The player's touchstone: "a
Pathfinder martial character dropped in a 5e world setting". **This world's magic, by
contrast, is potent** — its wizards, priests and great monsters are where real danger lives.

How the disparity shows up in play (to confirm in RULES_DELTAS.md at the end of intake):
ordinary natives are built low (level −1 to 2 published stat blocks, reskinned); crowds of them
fall fast; the encounter budget is spent on native casters, monsters and the occasional
champion, so fights stay real while the protagonist still feels enormous.

## Party structure

- **Characters the player controls:** 1 to start
- **Characters the GM runs:** 0 to start
- **Ally status:** none yet — the arc is planned, not given:
  1. **Solo** at the start, with the safety nets in RULES_DELTAS.md.
  2. **Quickly earns a simple companion NPC** (a sidekick: one job, few decisions).
  3. That companion **grows into a full GM-run NPC**, built as a full PC.
  4. Eventually **a small squad the player leads**, making the strategic decisions and the
     tactical calls; the GM runs the individuals' personalities, the player commands.
     Mechanics for the squad (troupe play vs. commanded allies) to be decided when it arrives.

## Setting assumptions kept

| Assumption | Decision |
|---|---|
| Gods and religion | An **original pantheon**, with obvious nods to real myths. Each culture has its own few most important gods, plus a smattering of widely important gods that most states worship to some degree. They range from lawful good to chaotic evil, and each nation's people and actions reflect its gods' alignments. The gods of the protagonist's home world have **counterparts here, or are replaced by these gods, or have no representation at all**. The peace between the gods is fragile. |
| Planes and afterlife | Not yet decided. It matters only once world-hopping comes up. |
| Ancestries present | Human-majority, with dwarves present (the protagonist is probably one or the other). Other peoples to be decided at world-build. |
| Magic prevalence | **Varies by nation.** The most advanced and most lawful states concentrate and regulate magic in churches and academies. Some lands disavow and distrust magic because of the evil it brought them. Everyone knows magic exists and can do great and terrible things; what a given person knows depends on where they grew up. Native magic is **potent**. |
| Technology level | Medieval, with **basic firearms and crossbows that vary by region**. |

## Themes to keep returning to

- **Disparity.** The stranger is a near-demigod among ordinary people, yet outclassed by this
  world's magic and its great monsters.
- **A world on a knife-edge.** Every victory moves a domino.
- **Earning a name in a world that has never heard of you.**
